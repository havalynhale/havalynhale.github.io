Introduction to Radio Frequency Parameters Poster

Independent project, presented at the Quantitative Engineering Analysis (QEA) final event. Fall 2025.

This is a fun project I took on to challenge myself. I did all the research myself and compiled it into one poster that was printed and presented to students and teachers alike. I had to be conscious about making it beginner friendly, as the audience was mostly my fellow undergraduates!

Worked Smith chart example

I worked this example by hand: a 43 + j15 Ω load on a 50 Ω line. Normalized, that's z = 0.86 + j0.3. I plotted it on the Smith chart and calculated its reflection coefficient, which shows the load is close to matched but slightly inductive.

My hand-worked example from the poster, with a sketch of what's happening physically on the line.

What I'd correct now

I've left the poster as I presented it, but looking back, there are a few things I'd fix:

  • Y parameter equations: I wrote I1 = Y11I1 + Y12I2. The right side should use voltages: I1 = Y11V1 + Y12V2.
  • S12: a lower |S12| means better isolation between the ports, not a higher one.
  • S21: this is the forward gain from input to output. My paragraph described signal being fed back to the input, which is reverse transmission (S12).
  • Lossy vs lossless: I mixed up the two when I said the lossy option sounds better. The audio comparison also blurs two different ideas: a lossless network (|S11|² + |S21|² = 1) and lossless audio compression.
  • Inductive impedance: current lags voltage because of inductors, not resistors.
  • Transmission lines: my photo and description were of power lines. In RF, transmission lines are things like coaxial cable and microstrip traces.
  • Smith chart math: for z = 0.86 + j0.3, S11 works out to about −0.048 + j0.169, so |S11| ≈ 0.18 rather than 0.165. The conclusion (close to matched, slightly inductive) still holds.
